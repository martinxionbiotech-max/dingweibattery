# Battery supplier verification

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-10 · Sources: National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, IATF 16949 certification rules

A supplier’s brochure tells you what it wants to be. Verification tells you what it is. Before you pay a deposit, three things must check out independently: the legal entity, the certificates, and the claimed capacity.

**Short answer:** Verify a battery supplier in three layers before you pay: (1) the legal entity — check the 18-character unified social credit code on the business licence against gsxt.gov.cn to confirm it is a factory, not a trading company; (2) the certificates — an IATF 16949 certificate runs a 3-year cycle and can be suspended, so check the number with the certifier; (3) the capacity — daily output is only believable when casting capacity, formation circuits and headcount agree. A trading company fronting as a factory adds 5–15% and removes your quality control.

## Layer 1 — Verify the legal entity

Every legitimate Chinese company has a business licence carrying a unified social credit code (统一社会信用代码) — an 18-character alphanumeric identifier. That code is the key to the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System ([gsxt.gov.cn](https://www.gsxt.gov.cn/index.html)), a free public register maintained by the State Administration for Market Regulation.

Look up the code and confirm four fields against what the supplier told you:

-   **Business scope** — does it say production and manufacturing (生产/制造), or only trading (贸易)? A trading company cannot legally run a battery production line, yet many present themselves as factories.
-   **Registered capital** — Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd.’s registered capital is RMB 50 million. A “factory” with registered capital of RMB 100,000 is a red flag that it cannot fund a production line.
-   **Legal representative and registered address** — these should match the licence you were sent and the address you will ship to.
-   **Operating status** — the register shows whether the entity is in normal operation, revoked, or subject to enforcement.

A supplier that will not share its unified social credit code before an order will not share anything useful after it. There is no legitimate reason to hide the licence.

## Layer 2 — Verify the certificates

Certificates are point-in-time documents, and they can be expired, suspended or simply faked. Do not accept a certificate image; ask for the number and check it.

| Certificate | What it covers | How to check it |
|---|---|---|
| IATF 16949 | Automotive quality management system | Confirm the number and scope with the issuing certification body (see [IATF Global Oversight](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/)). |
| ISO 9001 | General quality management | Verify on the certifier’s register; confirm the scope actually includes batteries. |
| ISO 45001 / OHSAS 18001 | Occupational health and safety | Check the certificate number and validity window. |
| Market certs (CE, SASO, etc.) | Destination-market conformity | Confirm the standard and the notified body or issuing authority. |

An IATF 16949 certificate is issued for a 3-year cycle with annual surveillance audits. A certificate can therefore be _within_ its printed expiry date and still be suspended for a failed audit — the paper date is not proof of status. The number is what lets you confirm current standing. Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. holds IATF 16949, ISO 9001, ISO 45001, OHSAS 18001 and can supply the certificate numbers on request, the same as any candidate you should be willing to verify.

## Layer 3 — Verify the claimed capacity

Capacity claims are the easiest number to inflate and the hardest for a buyer to test from a desk. The trick is that a real production line has bottlenecks, and the bottlenecks have to agree with each other. Three numbers must be consistent:

-   **Casting capacity** — how many grids per day the casting machines produce, which caps plate output.
-   **Formation capacity** — how many cells the formation (charging) circuits can process at once, multiplied by cycles per day. Formation is usually the real bottleneck.
-   **Headcount** — the number of line workers, which has to support the claimed throughput.

If a factory claims 40,000 batteries a day but its formation area holds a fraction of that, the claim is marketing. Ask for the three numbers separately and check they multiply out. The [factory audit guide](/knowledge/how-audit-battery-factory/) shows you what to look at on site to confirm this.

## The verification checklist

| Item | What to request | Pass signal |
|---|---|---|
| Business licence | Unified social credit code | Matches gsxt.gov.cn; scope is production. |
| Registered capital | From the register | Consistent with running a production line. |
| IATF 16949 / ISO 9001 | Certificate number + scope | Verifiable with the certifier. |
| Capacity | Casting + formation + headcount | The three numbers agree. |
| Ownership | Factory address vs registered address | They match; a video walkthrough is offered. |
| Export record | Bill of lading sample or customer reference | They can name destination markets they serve. |

## What fakes look like

-   **Borrowed photos** — a trading company photographs another factory’s line. A live video call with a specific instruction (hold up today’s date) separates the owner from the tourist.
-   **Expired or suspended certificates** — the paper looks current, the number does not check out.
-   **Inflated capacity** — the brochure claims a number the formation area cannot support.
-   **A mailbox address** — the registered address is an office, not the plant you are told exists.

None of these requires an on-site visit to catch. Two of the three layers — the licence and the certificates — verify from a desk; only capacity truly benefits from eyes on the line.

## Where verification fits in the sequence

Verification sits between the shortlist and the audit. It is cheap and fast — an hour of register checks — and it removes the candidates that would waste a full audit. Run it, then audit the survivors, then issue the [RFQ](/knowledge/oem-battery-rfq-guide/).
