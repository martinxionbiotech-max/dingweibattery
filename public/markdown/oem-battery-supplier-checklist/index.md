# OEM battery supplier checklist

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-01 · Source: Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. technical documentation

Run a supplier through these checks before you commit to an OEM battery order.

**Short answer:** The red flag that should stop you immediately is a quote given before the standard family and CCA basis are fixed — a supplier that prices a “72 Ah battery” without asking which standard is guessing, and so is the price. A checklist is a filter, not a guarantee.

## Specification

-   Standard family confirmed: JIS, DIN, BCI, AS or SA.
-   Voltage, capacity (Ah) and CCA agreed in writing.
-   Case size, terminal type and polarity defined.
-   Reference model or drawing attached to the order.

## Certifications

Request current certificates rather than marketing claims. Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. holds IATF 16949, ISO 9001, ISO 45001, OHSAS 18001.

## Factory capability

-   Factory area and number of production lines.
-   Daily output versus your annual volume.
-   Export experience and destination markets.

Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. operates a 200,000 m² plant with 18 automated lines and exports to 70+ countries.

## Commercial terms

-   MOQ: 1 × 20ft container (approx. 600–800 units, model dependent).
-   Lead time: 30–45 days after order confirmation.
-   Payment: T/T (30% deposit, 70% before shipment) or L/C at sight.
-   Incoterms: FOB Tianjin / CFR / CIF.
-   Warranty: 12 months from bill of lading date.

## Repeatability

Confirm how the manufacturer keeps subsequent orders identical to the approved sample — label, packaging, specification and quality.

## Red flags

-   No current certificates when you ask for them.
-   Quoting before the standard family and CCA basis are fixed.
-   No sample available from a recent production run.

If any of these appear, slow the process down and re-check the [five-point decision framework](/knowledge/how-choose-oem-battery-manufacturer/) before placing volume.

## Check the quote, not just the price

A quote is a six-part scope. If any part is missing, the number is not comparable:

-   **Tooling costs** — one-time or phased, and refundable or not.
-   **MOQ structure** — prototype / pilot / production clearly separated.
-   **Test report scope** — transport, safety and performance reports listed.
-   **Warranty terms** — duration, exclusions and failure handling.
-   **Engineering support** — included or separately charged.
-   **Change control** — a formal ECO process, not a verbal agreement.

Use the same six fields when you issue the [OEM battery RFQ](/knowledge/oem-battery-rfq-guide/) so suppliers are forced to quote on scope, not on a bare unit number.
