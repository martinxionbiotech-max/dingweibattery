# China battery industry regions

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-10 · Source: industry cluster research; verify individual factories independently

China’s lead-acid battery industry is not one market — it is five or six regional clusters, each with its own product mix, cost structure and export posture. Shortlisting by region gets you a tighter, more comparable set of candidates than shortlisting by search ranking.

**Short answer:** China’s lead-acid battery production clusters in roughly 5 regions — Hebei (Jinzhou/Shijiazhuang, where Dingwei’s parent Chengguang Power Tech is based), Shandong (Linyi/Weifang), Jiangsu, Zhejiang (Changxing, the traditional domestic heartland) and Anhui (Jieshou, a recycled-lead centre). Zhejiang’s Changxing hosts 2 of China’s largest domestic producers, but a cluster is not a quality grade — every cluster contains both export-grade plants and low-end rebuilders, so verify each factory individually.

## Why the industry clusters

Lead-acid production clusters for three structural reasons that have nothing to do with marketing. First, the supply chain is heavy and local: recycled lead smelting, sulphuric acid, separators and grid alloy all want to sit close to the plant to keep freight down. Second, the workforce is specialised — a region builds a pool of plate-casting and formation operators that a new entrant can hire on day one. Third, environmental permitting is easier inside a designated industrial park, which is where the clusters live.

For a buyer, the practical consequence is that two factories an hour apart share suppliers and labour but can sit a full price tier apart. The region narrows the field; it does not grade the players.

## The major clusters

| Region | Key areas | What it is known for | Buyer note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hebei | Jinzhou, Shijiazhuang belt | Export-focused OEM and private-label production; deep automotive battery experience | Chengguang Power Tech is based here — an example of the region’s export posture. |
| Shandong | Linyi, Weifang | Large-scale lead recycling and mid-volume production | Strong on recycled-lead supply; verify the grade of lead, not just the volume. |
| Jiangsu | Various industrial parks | Higher-cost, process-driven plants; export and OEM friendly | Often strong on documentation and certification discipline. |
| Zhejiang | Changxing (Huzhou) | The traditional domestic heartland; large branded producers | Home to major domestic brands; the biggest names may be less interested in small OEM runs. |
| Anhui | Jieshou (Tianying) | Recycled-lead smelting and battery reconditioning | Strong on cost and material recovery; inspect process control carefully. |

## How to use the region in shortlisting

The region is a filter, not a verdict. Use it in three ways:

1.  **Match the product to the region** — if you need a big-brand domestic supplier, Zhejiang; if you need an export OEM partner willing to run private label, the Hebei/Jiangsu posture often fits better; if cost on recycled lead matters most, look at Shandong or Anhui.
2.  **Compare within a region first** — two factories sharing a lead supplier and a labour pool quote more comparably than factories 1,000 km apart. An apples-to-apples RFQ comparison is easier inside one cluster.
3.  **Never let the region do the verifying** — the same cluster contains export-grade plants and rebuilders. Run the [supplier verification](/knowledge/battery-supplier-verification/) on every candidate regardless of postcode.

## What the region does not tell you

A cluster raises the floor on infrastructure — the roads, the recycled-lead supply, the permit environment — but it raises nothing about your specific supplier. The mistakes buyers make are the reverse of what they expect:

-   **Assuming a famous cluster means quality** — the weak plants inside a strong cluster still exist, and they price accordingly.
-   **Assuming a less famous cluster means weak quality** — some of the most disciplined export plants sit outside the headline regions.
-   **Paying a cluster premium without knowing it** — brand-name regions can carry a price premium that does not show up in the specification.

The only reliable way to separate the two is the same three-layer check everywhere: the business licence, the certificates and the capacity — covered in the [supplier verification](/knowledge/battery-supplier-verification/) guide.

## Region, then specification, then price

The sourcing order matters. Choose the region to build a credible shortlist, lock the specification in the [specifications reference](/knowledge/battery-specifications-guide/), then score candidates with the [how to choose a manufacturer](/knowledge/how-choose-oem-battery-manufacturer/) framework. Only after that does price become meaningful — comparing prices across regions without a fixed specification is comparing different batteries.
