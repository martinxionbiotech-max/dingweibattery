# OEM vs ODM vs private label

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-01 · Source: Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. technical documentation

All three models let you sell batteries under your own brand. The difference is how much of the specification and design you control.

**Short answer:** The three models differ by who owns the design: OEM means you own it, ODM means the factory owns it, private label means you brand an existing product. Getting it wrong costs you either design ownership you wanted, or development fees you did not need.

## Definitions

-   **OEM** — you define the specification and branding; the manufacturer builds to your requirements.
-   **ODM** — the manufacturer owns a ready design; you select it, adapt branding and optionally request changes.
-   **Private label** — a supplied product carries your name, label and packaging.

## Which one fits your sourcing goal

### Choose OEM

When you have a defined technical specification and need exact control.

### Choose ODM

When you want a proven platform and faster time to market.

### Choose private label

When the priority is brand identity on an existing product.

## What stays constant

Whichever path you choose, verify the standard family, voltage, capacity, CCA, dimensions, terminal layout, certifications and export terms before ordering.

## Which model fits you?

Choose by the asset you already have, not by the size of the first order:

-   **You have a specification or design capability** — choose OEM, because you control the product definition.
-   **You want a proven design and faster launch** — choose ODM.
-   **You want to test a market under your brand** — choose private label.

If the answer changes by model or market, run each line through the [OEM battery specification guide](/knowledge/oem-battery-specification-guide/) before splitting the program.

## The cost of getting it wrong

Choosing the wrong model costs twice: either you pay development work that should have been ODM, or you sign an ODM agreement expecting to own the design and later find the manufacturer owns it. The commercial fix is to state ownership in writing — not to assume the model name transfers the design.
