# Battery shipping, packaging and warranty

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-03 · Source: export logistics practice

A battery order is not complete when it leaves the line — it is complete when it arrives undamaged, with the right documents, and with a warranty you understand.

**Short answer:** Lead-acid batteries ship as ordinary cargo (not Class 9 dangerous goods like lithium), which simplifies transport. The key logistics points are container weight limits, export documentation, and a warranty whose scope — duration, exclusions and handling — is written down.

## Why lead-acid is simpler to ship

Unlike lithium cells, lead-acid starting batteries are not Class 9 dangerous goods. For importers this means fewer transport restrictions, no UN38.3 report, and a lighter documentation burden — a real logistics advantage of lead-acid for an OEM program.

## Packaging and container loading

Batteries are heavy, so the 20ft container often reaches its weight limit before its volume limit. This is why MOQ follows the container (typically 1 × 20ft container (approx. 600–800 units, model dependent)). Packaging must protect the terminals from shorting and the case from impact during handling.

-   Pallets and cartons sized to the container plan.
-   Terminal protection and upright orientation.
-   Mixed-model loading to fill a container efficiently and spread unit cost.

## Export documentation

A clean export set avoids delays at customs. Typical documents include:

-   Commercial invoice and packing list
-   Bill of lading
-   Certificate of origin
-   Test reports (CCA / capacity) where required

Shipping terms follow FOB Tianjin / CFR / CIF, with export from Tianjin / Shanghai.

## Warranty: read the scope, not just the number

Warranty is 12 months from bill of lading date. But a warranty is only as good as its written scope. Confirm three things:

1.  **Duration** — from bill of lading date.
2.  **Exclusions** — what is _not_ covered (deep discharge, misuse, physical damage).
3.  **Handling** — how a claim is filed, inspected and settled.

A “12-month warranty” with broad exclusions is not the same as a 12-month warranty with clear, narrow ones. Get the terms in writing.

## What buyers should ask

-   How are batteries packed and loaded — is the container weight-limited or volume-limited?
-   Which export documents are provided as standard?
-   What are the exact warranty exclusions and claim handling?
-   What Incoterm applies, and where does risk transfer?
