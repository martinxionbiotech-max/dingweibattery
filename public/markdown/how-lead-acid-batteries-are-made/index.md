# How lead-acid batteries are made

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-03 · Source: lead-acid manufacturing process

A lead-acid starting battery is built through ten stages, from casting the lead grid to final electrical testing. Understanding the stages tells a buyer what to look for when auditing a factory.

**Short answer:** Lead-acid battery manufacturing runs through ten stages, but three decide most of the quality: curing (2–4 days of crystallisation that gives the plates strength), paste mixing (consistent paste means consistent capacity), and formation charging (the first charge that activates the material). A factory that rushes these three ships batteries that measure fine on day one and fade early.

## The ten stages

1.  **Grid casting** — molten lead alloy is cast into the grid frame. Maintenance-free batteries use a lead-calcium alloy that reduces water loss.
2.  **Paste mixing** — lead oxide is mixed into a paste; its consistency determines the active material’s capacity.
3.  **Pasting** — the paste is applied evenly onto the grid.
4.  **Curing** — the pasted plates cure for 2–4 days, developing the crystal structure that gives mechanical strength.
5.  **Assembly** — positive and negative plates are stacked with separators and inserted into the case.
6.  **Welding** — plate lugs and terminals are welded to form the electrical path.
7.  **Electrolyte filling** — sulphuric acid electrolyte is added.
8.  **Formation charging** — the first charge converts the paste into active material.
9.  **Sealing & finishing** — the lid is sealed and the battery finished.
10.  **Testing & packaging** — 100% electrical testing before packing.

## Where quality is won or lost

Three stages have an outsized effect on the final battery:

-   **Curing** — a rushed cure produces weak plates that shed material and fail early. This is why curing time (2–4 days) is a meaningful audit question.
-   **Paste mixing** — inconsistent paste produces inconsistent capacity cell-to-cell.
-   **Formation** — incomplete formation leaves unformed active material, reducing both capacity and cycle life.

## What this means for a buyer

You do not need to run the line — you need to know which questions separate a disciplined plant from a rushed one. When auditing, ask about curing time, paste consistency control and formation completion, not just about capacity and price.

This is an editorial framework for evaluating manufacturing, not a technical specification.

## What buyers should ask

-   What is the grid alloy, and is it lead-calcium for maintenance-free?
-   How long are the plates cured?
-   How is paste consistency controlled?
-   Is formation charging completed before testing?
-   Is every battery electrically tested before packing?
