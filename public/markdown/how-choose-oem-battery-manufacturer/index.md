# How to choose an OEM battery manufacturer

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-01 · Source: Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. technical documentation

Choose a manufacturer that can match your standard, hold quality systems, and repeat your specification order after order.

**Short answer:** The single most important factor in choosing an OEM battery manufacturer is repeatability — whether the tenth container matches the first approved sample. Match the standard family, verify current certificates and check output against your volume, but a low price is worth nothing if the specification drifts between orders.

## 1\. Match the standard family

Your market determines the standard: JIS, DIN, BCI, AS or SA. Confirm the factory regularly produces that family rather than treating your order as a one-off.

## 2\. Check quality systems

Ask which certifications the manufacturer holds. For automotive supply, IATF 16949 is the relevant quality system; ISO 9001 covers general quality management.

Look at plant size, line count, daily output, export experience and whether the facility can support your volume. Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. operates a 200,000 m² facility with 18 automated lines, producing 40,000 batteries per day and exporting to 70+ countries.

## 4\. Define the specification before quoting

Fix voltage, capacity, CCA, case size, terminal and polarity first. Ambiguous specifications produce samples that cannot be fairly compared.

## A five-point decision framework

Score each manufacturer against the same five questions instead of comparing marketing claims.

1.  **Specification** — “Which standards and models do you produce in-house?”
2.  **Certification** — “Can you share current IATF 16949 / ISO 9001 certificates?”
3.  **Scale** — “What is your daily output and how does it map to my annual volume?”
4.  **Repeatability** — “How do you lock a specification so repeat orders match?”
5.  **Export** — “Which markets do you ship to, and what documents do you provide?”

Combine this framework with the [OEM battery specification guide](/knowledge/oem-battery-specification-guide/) so you are scoring the same requirements you will actually order.

## Questions that expose a weak supplier

-   They cannot name the CCA test method.
-   They quote before confirming the standard family.
-   They cannot show a sample from a recent production run.

Run each shortlisted factory through the [OEM battery supplier checklist](/knowledge/oem-battery-supplier-checklist/) before you commit volume.

## Weight the five factors

Do not choose on one number. Score every shortlisted manufacturer against the same five factors and total the result.

| Factor | Weight | What to score |
|---|---|---|
| Specification coverage | 25% | Can they produce the standard family and models you need? |
| Certifications | 25% | Are current IATF 16949 / ISO 9001 certificates available? |
| Scale | 20% | Does daily output map to your annual volume? |
| Repeatability | 20% | Is there a locked specification and sample process? |
| Export experience | 10% | Which markets do they serve and document? |

## Lead-acid-specific checks

For lead-acid, the highest-risk checks are process checks, not marketing checks:

-   **Grid alloy** — maintenance-free batteries normally use lead-calcium grids; confirm the alloy is stated.
-   **Paste consistency** — ask how paste is controlled between batches.
-   **Curing** — verify the plate curing window is sufficient, normally 2–4 days.
-   **Formation** — confirm the battery is fully formed and 100% end-of-line tested.
