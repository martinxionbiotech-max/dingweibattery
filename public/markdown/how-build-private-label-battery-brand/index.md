# How to build a private-label battery brand

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-01 · Source: Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. technical documentation

A private-label battery brand starts with a positioning decision, not a label design. Here is the sequence.

**Short answer:** The logo is the last step, not the first — fix the specification and positioning before you design the label, or you are branding a product you cannot yet reproduce. Then plan SKUs across your market’s standard family, not a single container.

## 1\. Pick the position

Decide the market and segment: passenger car, truck, heavy-duty, start-stop. The position determines the standard (JIS, DIN, BCI) and the specification.

## 2\. Fix the specification

Lock voltage, capacity, CCA, case size and terminal against your reference model. Confirm the standard family for your target market.

## 3\. Brand it

-   Label artwork
-   Packaging design
-   Barcode and SKU planning
-   Warranty terms

## 4\. Launch it

Order a container, plan the logistics, and put the documentation set in place (COA, traceability, test reports).

## Positioning before branding

Choose one position before you design a label: value, specification quality, or a specific vehicle class. A brand that tries to mean everything to every buyer usually has no reason to be chosen in any segment.

## Plan SKUs, not one container

A private-label brand is a SKU plan across models, not a single first order. Start with the target market’s standard family — JIS, DIN or BCI — then list the SKUs you need before negotiating the first container.

-   List the standard family and reference models.
-   Group SKUs by terminal, capacity and application.
-   Forecast each SKU to avoid one-container overbuying.
-   Keep label and packaging common so later SKUs launch faster.

Turn that SKU list into a sourcing plan with the [OEM battery RFQ guide](/knowledge/oem-battery-rfq-guide/).

## The positioning matrix

Place your brand on a simple 2×2 before spending on packaging: price on one axis and vehicle class on the other — passenger, commercial or heavy-duty. Choose one cell and defend it; a brand that tries to occupy every cell ends up with no defensible position.

Once the cell is chosen, keep the line aligned by revisiting the [private-label battery strategy](/knowledge/private-label-battery-strategy/) before each new SKU.
