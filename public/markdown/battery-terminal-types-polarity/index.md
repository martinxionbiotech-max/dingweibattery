# Battery terminal types and polarity

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-03 · Source: battery standards documentation

A battery can match the right standard and case size and still not fit — because the terminal type, position or polarity is wrong. Terminal detail is a fitment decision, not an afterthought.

**Short answer:** A battery with the right case size and capacity can still be the wrong battery if the terminal type, position or polarity is off — terminal detail is a fitment decision, not an afterthought. All three must be confirmed against the vehicle before ordering.

## Terminal type

Different markets use different post designs. Common automotive types include the tapered top post (most JIS/DIN/BCI cars), the side terminal (some North American GM applications), and stud/flag terminals used on heavy-duty and marine batteries.

A terminal that does not match the vehicle cable clamp will not connect securely — this is a physical compatibility issue, not a performance issue.

## Terminal position

The same battery model can ship with the positive post on the left or the right. Manufacturers often denote this with an L or R suffix (for example, a D23L versus a D23R). The position matters because the vehicle’s cable lengths are fixed.

## Polarity

Polarity is the assignment of positive and negative to the two posts. Reversing polarity on connection can damage the vehicle’s electrical system, so polarity is confirmed against the vehicle wiring before fitment.

## Why these are confirmed on inquiry

On Dingwei reference pages, terminal type and polarity are marked “confirm on inquiry” rather than guessed. That is deliberate: a wrong terminal specification turns a correct battery into a wrong battery. The manufacturer confirms terminal detail against the buyer’s vehicle or fleet requirement.

## What buyers should ask

-   What terminal type does the vehicle use — top post, side terminal or stud?
-   Is the positive post on the left or right (L/R)?
-   What is the correct polarity for the vehicle wiring?
-   Does the heavy-duty application need dual or flag terminals?

Confirm terminals together with [dimensions and case size](/knowledge/battery-dimensions-case-size/), then lock them into the [OEM specification](/knowledge/oem-battery-specification-guide/).
