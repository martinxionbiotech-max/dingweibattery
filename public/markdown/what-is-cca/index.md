# What is CCA (cold cranking amps)?

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-03 · Source: battery test standards

CCA measures how much current a battery can deliver for 30 seconds at a cold temperature before dropping below a cut-off voltage. It is the number most buyers compare — and the number most often compared incorrectly.

**Short answer:** Cold cranking amps (CCA) is the current a fully charged battery can deliver for 30 seconds at a defined cold temperature while maintaining a minimum voltage. Because the temperature and voltage thresholds differ between standards, a JIS CCA and an EN CCA are not the same number for the same battery.

## What CCA actually measures

CCA answers one question: will this battery start the engine in cold weather? A higher CCA means more current available to the starter motor at low temperature, where engine oil is thicker and the cranking demand is highest.

It is a starting-power metric, not a capacity metric. A battery can have a high CCA and a modest amp-hour (Ah) capacity, or the reverse — the two measure different things.

## Why one battery has two CCA numbers

CCA is not a single universal test. The main standards measure at different temperatures:

| Standard | Test temperature | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| JIS | −15°C | Japanese / Asian-market batteries |
| EN | −18°C | European batteries |
| SAE | −18°C | North American batteries |
| DIN | −18°C | German / European batteries |

Because JIS tests at a warmer −15°C, the same battery can show a different CCA number under JIS than under EN/SAE. The two are not interchangeable and there is no universal percentage conversion.

## How to compare CCA correctly

1.  **Confirm the test basis first** — JIS, EN, SAE or DIN. Ask the supplier which standard the number refers to.
2.  **Compare within one standard** — only compare JIS with JIS, EN with EN.
3.  **Match the cut-off voltage** — some standards use different end voltages, which shifts the result.
4.  **Treat the number as a reference** — the final CCA for a specific battery is confirmed by the manufacturer against its test procedure.

On Dingwei reference pages, CCA is shown as a **(reference)** value. Confirm the final CCA and its test basis with the manufacturer before using it in a product specification.

## What buyers should ask

-   Which standard is this CCA measured to — JIS, EN, SAE or DIN?
-   At what temperature and cut-off voltage?
-   Is this a nominal value or a tested value from a specific production batch?
-   How does CCA relate to the capacity (Ah) you are quoting?

## CCA vs Ah: do not confuse them

Ah (amp-hours) measures stored energy over time — how long the battery can supply current. CCA measures the peak current burst for starting. A vehicle with high electrical accessories needs Ah; a cold-weather diesel needs CCA. Most OEM specifications list both, and both must be confirmed.
