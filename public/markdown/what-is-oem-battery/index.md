# What is an OEM battery?

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-01 · Source: Chengguang Power Tech Co., Ltd. technical documentation

An OEM battery is a battery produced to the buyer’s specification and sold under the buyer’s brand — rather than the factory’s own label.

**Short answer:** An OEM battery is built to the buyer’s specification and sold under the buyer’s brand — quality is set by the specification, not the label. The same factory often builds branded and OEM batteries on the same line; the buyer’s spec decides the outcome.

## The core idea

OEM stands for original equipment manufacturer. In battery sourcing, OEM means the supplier manufactures to an agreed specification: voltage, capacity, CCA, standard family, case size, terminal layout and labelling. The buyer controls the product definition and brand.

## How OEM differs from ODM and private label

-   **OEM** — you bring the specification; the manufacturer builds to it.
-   **ODM** — the manufacturer offers an existing design you can brand and adjust.
-   **Private label** — your identity is applied to a supplied product platform.

## What goes into an OEM battery specification

At minimum, an OEM battery project defines the standard family (JIS, DIN, BCI, AS or SA), voltage, capacity (Ah), CCA, dimensions, terminal type and polarity. Reference models such as 65D26 or H6 are often used as a starting point.

## OEM, ODM and private label: who owns what?

In procurement terms, the three models differ by who owns the design and who owns the brand. Keep that ownership split visible before signing a sourcing agreement.

| Model | Who owns design | Who owns brand | Typical buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| OEM | Buyer | Buyer | Buyer has a defined specification |
| ODM | Manufacturer | Buyer | Buyer wants a proven platform |
| Private label | Manufacturer | Buyer | Buyer wants fastest market entry |

If your requirements sit between two models, use the [full OEM vs ODM vs private-label decision guide](/knowledge/oem-vs-odm-vs-private-label/) before committing.

## Why lead-acid OEM is different from lithium

Lead-acid is a 150-year-old supply chain. Unlike lithium cells, lead-acid starting batteries are not Class 9 dangerous goods, so shipping and compliance are simpler. For importers, this means fewer transport restrictions and lower documentation burden.

That transport reality is a procurement advantage, not only a technical detail: it simplifies carrier selection and reduces the document set that can delay a container. Review the same supply chain in a factory context with the [lead-acid factory audit](/knowledge/how-audit-battery-factory/).
