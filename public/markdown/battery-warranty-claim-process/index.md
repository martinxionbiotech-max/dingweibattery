# Battery warranty claim process

Published by Dingwei Battery · Last reviewed 2026-09-10 · Sources: industry warranty practice, Midtronics pro-rated coverage explanation

A warranty is only worth what you can claim. Most battery buyers read the duration and skip the claim mechanics — then discover, at the worst possible moment, that the settlement is pro-rata, the exclusions are broad, or the documentation they did not keep is the evidence they now need. This page walks how a claim actually works, so you can file one that succeeds.

**Short answer:** A battery warranty claim runs through three steps — prove the failure with a test report, file against the written scope (duration, exclusions, handling), and settle as either full replacement or pro-rata credit based on the battery's age. The factory term is typically 12 months from bill of lading; retail warranties often run 12–36 months free replacement before shifting to pro-rata, and the exclusions are where most claims are refused.

## What the warranty actually covers

Before you can claim, you have to know the scope. A battery warranty has three written parts, and only the first is the headline number:

1.  **Duration** — the factory term is 12 months from bill of lading date. The clock starts at the bill of lading date, not at your customer's purchase.
2.  **Exclusions** — deep discharge, misuse, physical damage, incorrect charging and sulfation from long storage are the standard exclusions.
3.  **Handling** — how a failure is filed, inspected and settled.

The full reading of a warranty scope is in the [shipping and warranty](/knowledge/battery-shipping-warranty/) page.

## Full replacement vs pro-rata: the settlement that surprises people

The industry settlement pattern is two-tiered. Inside the **free-replacement window** — commonly the first 12–24 months for retail brands — a defective battery is replaced free. After that window, the warranty shifts to **pro-rata**: you receive a credit for a fraction of the battery's value based on its age, not a full refund (see \[Midtronics' explanation of pro-rated coverage\](https://www.midtronics.com/blog/how-to-explain-battery-warranties-and-pro-rated-coverage)). The implication is uncomfortable and important: a "36-month warranty" that is pro-rata after 24 months can be worth less in a claim than a full-replacement 12-month warranty — and the lead-acid industry's own body, \[Battery Council International\](https://batterycouncil.org/sources), documents the recycling economics that make short full-replacement windows a fair, sustainable default. When you compare suppliers, compare the free-replacement window, not the total duration.

## The claim process, step by step

1.  **Measure the failure** — a CCA or capacity test that shows the battery is below spec. A claim without a measured failure is a claim that gets refused.
2.  **Assemble the evidence** — invoice, bill of lading, test report and photos.
3.  **File against the written scope** — match the failure to a covered cause, because the exclusions are where claims die.
4.  **Await inspection** — the factory or seller reviews the test data and the claim against the exclusions.
5.  **Settle** — full replacement, or pro-rata credit calculated from the battery's age.

Realistically, a documented claim settles in about 2–4 weeks; a claim without documentation can stall indefinitely. The evidence you keep at delivery is the difference.

## The exclusions, and why they decide most claims

-   **Deep discharge** — a battery drained below about 50% charge and left there sulfates; that is a use failure, not a defect.
-   **Misuse and wrong application** — a battery spec'd for the wrong vehicle or duty cycle.
-   **Physical damage** — cracked cases, crushed terminals, shorted handling damage.
-   **Long storage without maintenance** — a battery that self-discharged on the shelf for a year is usually excluded, which is why storage discipline matters.

This is the quiet link between warranty and warehousing: a battery that sits too long before sale has already used its warranty clock and may have begun sulfating. The [storage and shelf life](/knowledge/battery-storage-shelf-life/) page explains how to keep stock claimable.

## How to make a claim succeed

-   **Test before you file.** A CCA or capacity reading that shows the failure is the single most persuasive piece of evidence.
-   **Keep the delivery documents.** Invoice and bill of lading anchor the warranty start date.
-   **Quote the scope back.** Reference the written duration, exclusions and handling when you file.
-   **Act inside the window.** A claim filed the day after the window closes is refused on timing, not merit.
